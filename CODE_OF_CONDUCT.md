# Code of conduct

THY666 is made for people who want to keep an instrument alive and explore what
it can become. Musicians, firmware developers, first-time contributors and
curious tinkerers belong here. You do not need to know C, own a particular setup
or agree with every design choice to take part.

The project runs on spite, coffee and tokens. The spite is aimed at limitations,
not at the people working through them.

## How we work together

- Discuss the idea, the sound or the code. Disagree directly and explain why;
  do not turn criticism into an attack on someone's intelligence or identity.
- Make room for questions and different experience levels. A clear bug report,
  a careful listening test and a documentation correction all count as work.
- Be honest about evidence. Say what you observed, what you inferred and what
  remains untested. Nobody owes the project their board as a test fixture.
- AI assistance is welcome. The person submitting a contribution remains
  responsible for understanding it, checking it and answering review questions.
  Do not use generated volume to drown out a discussion or evade feedback.
- Respect privacy and boundaries. Ask before sharing someone else's messages,
  photos or identifying information. Redact device IDs and personal data in logs.

Harassment, discriminatory abuse, threats, sexual harassment, doxxing, sustained
personal attacks and retaliation against someone raising a concern are not
acceptable. Neither are repeated unwanted contact or deliberate disruption.

## Where this applies

This applies to project issues, pull requests, discussions and other spaces
maintained for THY666, including interactions where someone explicitly represents
the project. Maintainers follow the same expectations as contributors.

## Reporting a concern

Use GitHub's **Report content** or **Report abuse** controls on the relevant
content or account. These reports go to GitHub; they are not a private inbox
for THY666 maintainers. See [GitHub's reporting guide](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam).

For a public moderation request, you may ask a maintainer to review a thread
without repeating abusive material or identifying the person affected. Do not
post sensitive evidence in an issue. If a concern involves a maintainer, use
GitHub's reporting tools rather than relying on that person to handle it.

## Moderation

Maintainers may ask for a change in behavior, edit or remove content, lock a
thread, or restrict participation temporarily or permanently. The response
should reflect the severity and pattern of behavior; serious abuse can warrant
immediate removal. When appropriate, explain the decision and how constructive
participation can resume, without exposing private reports or affected people.

Good-faith mistakes can be corrected. Repeated boundary violations are not a
condition others must accept to participate here.
